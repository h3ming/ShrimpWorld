Apparently I need to stir the gravel cap around to let the air bubbles escape if I want this to happen in less than 24 hours. It so happens that planting moves the gravel around a lot too, which is how I found this information out. Oops. 

Preparing for this day, I had tried to plan my tank out a few different ways. I attempted to sketch out a few designs, but frankly I've never been good at plants and couldn't imagine what they would look like/how it would turn out. I also tried a couple of tank planning/decorating software/webapps, but they were finicky and a bit difficult to use. 
I had a nice rock I knew I wanted as my main hardscape, so I started with that, placing it on the left front side of the tank. I like how flat the bottom is, the white streak that goes accross the front, and the way it slopes up while forming something like a stage. I picked it up in a river during a trip to Vancouver last summer with my family and brought it back with me to Chicago last fall with the intention of putting it in a shrimp ecosystem. Today, it fulfilled its destiny.

I laid my plants out and meticulously researched their adult forms on Google Images before kind of giving up and just planting them in ways I thought might look good. I framed the rock with interesting-texured lobelia cardinalis, and the crypt reminded me a bit of a tree so I put it behind the rock for "shade". Ludwigis ovalis had a unique shape and I read it would have a redder future hue than the other plans, so I put it in the middle as a centrepiece. I spaced out the remaining plants from yesterday's bundle along the sides, and filled out the spaces in between with pearlweed. I left the foreground clear for the moss and open observation space.

At this point I was ready to add in the moss, but realized I didn't think through how to attach it. As a sidenote, I learned that subwassertang moss is not actually moss but a type of liverwort. Without being anchored properly, this plant would fall apart and disperse through the tank. The best way to plant moss is to superglue or tie it onto hardscape. It took me a while but I eventually found a small rock in my backpack (don't know how long it's been there) I could use. I didn't have any aquarium safe superglue and burn tested 3 spools of thread before remembering I have multiple skeins of 100% cotton yarn from my crochet hobby. I sectioned out a ply of green yarn and tied the subwassertang onto the small rock, and placed it in the right foreground of the tank. 

{% include slideshow.html photos=page.planting2 %}

I enlisted the help of my partner to carefully move the shallow now-planted tank to its final position on the living room ladder shelf. This shelf originally belonged to some tenant a few apartment generations ago and I wasn't able to find its exact model online, so I was uncertain how much weight it could safely bear. A 5 gallon tank with substrate weighs up to 60 pounds; I, being much more than 60 pounds, did climb into this shelving level and awkwardly bounce a bit earlier in the day (with no accidents!) to make sure there wouldn't be any surprises down the road. 
With the tank settled in its nook, I carefully filled it up to full for the first time. I used a plastic measuring cup, scooping from a bucket filled with conditioned tap water. If I poured too quickly, it would disturb the substrate and uproot plants. Planting them back in the tight space was much more unwieldy than on the kitchen counter. 
Once the tank was filled, I dropped in the salvinia and water lettuce floating plants and refit my lighting on the tank.
