Since my water readings seemed mostly okay, I reached out to the dwarf shrimp seller from a few days ago to see if the shrimp were still available. I wasn't planning on getting them so soon, but they told me someone had already picked up the first batch but they had one left, and the movers were coming on Thurday (today was Tuesday) so if I could come tonight or tomorrow that would be best. So I decided to pull the trigger and pick them up today!

I did a 40% water change to bring down my nitrate levels in preparation for the shrimp. According to the test strips, I had somewhat high nitrate levels, but no nitrates or ammonia, signalling that my tank had fully completed the nitrogen cycle and was ready for livestock. According to my research, cycling usually takes a few weeks and again I don't really trust the strips, but the plants had been growing well and the snails were doing fine.

{% include slideshow.html photos=page.preshrimp %}

With some time to kill before the pickup, I looked at the seller's post again. I noticed a comment left by the same person who sold me the plant bundle last week; he must have been the party that picked up the first batch of shrimp. This was also when I realized that the posting very clearly said CRYSTAL RED SHRIMP and not dwarf shrimp, and maybe the shrimp in the picture had clearly whiter bodies and very different patterning than red rilis, as I had assumed they were.  
Let me explain. "Dwarf shrimp" is the general term for the small, freshwater shrimp popular in the hobby. Red rili shrimp are a kind of neocaridina shrimp, which, as previously mentioned, are generally hardier and prefer harder water -- ideal for Chicago tap water. Crystal red shrimp, on the other hand, are a type of caridina shrimp, which are much more sensitive to water parameters and prefer soft water. In fact, every link I clicked on emphasized this particular shrimp type's fragility and requirement for exact water parameters. Oh well!!

Anyway, I drove up to the South Loop around 8:30pm and met the seller in the lobby of his apartment. Just an observation, but everybody I've interacted with in this hobby so far, including Golden Aquarium's owner (but not the butch who sold me the tank) has been of East Asian ethnicity. He looked a little frazzled and nervously eyed the residential concierge during our exchange, explaining another buyer had just left and that officially the building wanted guests to come in through the back. He said he'd been using RO water (reverse osmosis water -- this type of water is very pure has zero dissolved solids) to keep the shrimp, and he hadn't tried keeping them in tap water before but they should probably be fine. He handed over 15 beautiful shrimp, nicely bagged, and I handed over $20 in cash so even if they die it doesn't really count (monetarily at least this is joke.). 

{% include slideshow.html photos=page.shrimpup %}

I stopped at a nearby and rather labyrinthinely located Petco (seriously, the entrance was underground and I went through two loops of liminal garage levels and it was pretty empty since they close at 9) and picked up some shrimp food, a small fish net, and aquarium baster. 

Back home, I used a deli container to prop up the shrimp bag and slowly drip acclimated them with the baster over the course of a couple of hours. Around 2:20 am, the shrimp went in. They went to work immediately, scuttling on leaves and tearing off bits of algae and dying plant matter. The biggest one can pick up whole granules of the gravel and sends out a strong stream of particles when it chews. There's a medium sized one that pauses a bit more than the others.
