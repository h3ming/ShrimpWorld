A couple hours before the pickup time, I’m wandering Marshall’s for possible tank backings or levelling materials. I didn’t realize this location was closing down. I pick through some drying mats, but can’t picture any of them looking good around the tank. The tank has a thin one built in anyway, so it's more for peace of mind.

We’re meeting at the Jewel by the Orange Line, and I get there early enough to do some grocery shopping. The layout of this store vaguely resembles the local Kroger back home in Michigan. He arrives just as I'm ready to check out. I don't have much practice meeting strangers from the internet, and I'm surprised at how easily we identify each other. I knew he was going to be wearing a red jacket, while he knew nothing of my physical appearance, yet we locked eyes almost immediately after he entered the store (I didn't know he was going to walk in). Maybe there was some sort of subtle tank cultivating enthusiast signal that was emmitted and we happened to catch each other's frequency. 
He hands me a white plastic bag (featurng a local asian bakery) containing a somewhat muddled but neatly labled ziplock bag of plant matter that must have survived the work day with him. I learn he's been keeping tanks since high school. He tells me to reach out on Reddit if I have any questions, he's pretty good at responding on there (true).

Back home, I'm very excited to finally have everything I need to start setting up the tank. Tonight, I'm just going to get the substrate in and fill it with some water to get it ready for planting tomorrow. 
I move the tank to my kitchen counter to work. The bag of gravel recommends washing off dust before using it, so I pour 80% of it out into a large metal bowl and run it under my faucet a few times until the water is clear. I take a sifter to my bag of dirt to get rid of large chunks and materials that would float. I add some of the fine dirt to the bottom of the tank. I add some dechlorinated tap water at this point, which I'm not sure is the best move, since it takes a while to absorb and dirties the sides of the tank makes it difficult to see the its real height level. I spread it out as evenly as I can and scoop handfuls of wet gravel onto the surface. Eventually, I end up with about 1 inch of dirt and 1.5 inches of gravel for my substrate; possibly on the thicker side for substrates, but I definitely won't have any problems with dirt escaping the gravel cap or nutrients in the long term. I very carefully fill up a few more inches of water and let the tank sit overnight to settle a bit and let air bubbles to escape.
